The robust trend analysis supports the endpoint findings. Australia’s household income increased by approximately USD 458 per person per year and employment by 0.358 percentage points per year. Both trends showed strong monotonic improvement (favourable Kendall’s $\tau=0.85$ and $0.75$; Holm-adjusted $p<0.0001$), although Australia ranked only around the 39th and 33rd percentiles among eligible comparator-country slopes. In contrast, lack of social support increased by 0.186 percentage points per year, indicating deterioration ($\tau=-0.87$, Holm-adjusted $p=0.0333$), while negative affect also increased by 0.174 points per year but showed weak evidence of a consistent monotonic trend ($\tau=-0.33$, Holm-adjusted $p=0.4694$). All four slope directions agreed with the endpoint analysis. Thus, the evidence indicates genuine material progress without comparable improvement in the selected social outcomes.

**limitation**:

The social estimates are based on only six independent pooled survey windows, and standard trend p-values do not explicitly account for temporal dependence. They are therefore interpreted as robustness evidence rather than causal or definitive confirmatory tests.
